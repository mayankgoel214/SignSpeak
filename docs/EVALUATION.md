# Evaluation

Generated by `ml/write_evaluation_doc.py` from `eval/results.json`, which is the
output of `ml/evaluate.py`. Nothing here is typed by hand.

## The headline

**92.2% accuracy on a signer the model has never seen**, averaged over
5 leave-one-signer-out folds covering 65,522 test samples across
24 letters. Per-fold accuracy ranges from 87.5% to 95.4%
(standard deviation 2.6%).

The same model and the same training code, measured on a random 80/20 split, scores
**98.7%** — 6.5 percentage points higher. That gap is the whole point of this document.

## Why two numbers

The dataset is 65,774 frames captured from 5 people in
continuous recording sessions. Consecutive frames of one person holding one letter are
near-duplicates of each other. A random split scatters those near-duplicates across the
train/test boundary, so most test images have a near-twin in training and the model is
being asked, in part, to recognise pictures it has already memorised.

Leave-one-signer-out removes that entirely: for each fold, every frame of the test person
is absent from training. It is the number that estimates what happens when a stranger opens
the page.

## Protocol

- **Dataset**: Surrey ASL Fingerspelling (Pugeault & Bowden, 2011) — <https://www.cvssp.org/FingerSpellingKinect2011/>
- **Images**: 65,774; MediaPipe found a hand in 65,522 of them (99.62%).
  The remainder are dropped, and the model is measured only on the rest — so these figures
  describe images in which a hand was found, not all images.
- **Classes**: 24 static letters (A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H, I, K, L, M, N, O, P, Q, R, S, T, U, V, W, X, Y). J and Z are
  defined by movement and are absent from this dataset and from this model.
- **Features**: 21 MediaPipe landmarks, wrist-centred, rotation-aligned, scale-normalized (63 dims)
- **Model**: two hidden layers, trained for 60 epochs at a fixed seed (20260829).
  No early stopping and no hyperparameter search, so nothing in the training procedure ever
  reads the test set.
- **Determinism**: re-running `ml/evaluate.py` from the cached landmarks reproduces
  `eval/results.json` byte for byte — same SHA-256, every digit of the confusion matrix
  included. Verified by an independent full re-run, not asserted from the presence of a seed.

### Reading the errors

- **R is the weakest letter at 70.5%**, and its dominant error is
  R → U (19.7% of all R).
  Crossed fingers and two raised fingers produce nearly the same 21 landmarks.
- **P and Q trade places in both directions.** The two signs are close to one hand shape at
  two orientations, and the feature transform deliberately rotates orientation away. That
  choice is what lets the model survive a stranger holding their wrist at a different angle;
  the P/Q confusion is the price of it. Both facts come from the same design decision.
- **E → O and N → M** are the other real pairs: in each case the difference is how far the
  fingers curl, which a landmark model sees only as small distances between joints.

## Is the confidence number worth anything

Measured under the same leave-one-signer-out protocol, over 65,522 predictions
on unseen people. The model is **under-confident by 3.3 points**:
it claims 88.9% on average and is right 92.2% of the time.
Expected calibration error 3.4%.

Understating is the safer direction to be wrong in, and it is not an accident: the loss
uses label smoothing, which exists to stop a network being maximally certain, and this is
what that looks like from the outside.

### The commit threshold

A letter is committed only while the model stays above a confidence floor. The page uses
0.70, which was picked by hand before any of this was measured and turns out to sit at a
reasonable knee:

| floor | frames kept | accuracy of those |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0.00 | 100.0% | 92.18% |
| 0.50 | 94.9% | 95.09% |
| 0.60 | 91.8% | 96.19% |
| 0.70 ← in use | 88.1% | 97.20% |
| 0.80 | 83.3% | 98.07% |
| 0.90 | 74.1% | 98.97% |
| 0.95 | 59.3% | 99.57% |

Discarding 12% of frames buys five points of accuracy; going further to 0.90 costs another
14% of frames for under two. Reproduce with `python ml/measure_calibration.py`.

## Honest limits

- Five signers is a small population. The per-fold spread is the best available evidence of
  how much the figure moves between people, and it moves by several points.
- 0.38% of images produce no hand detection and are excluded. In the browser
  this shows up as the readout going blank rather than as a wrong letter.
- These are cropped, well-lit, single-hand images from a Kinect. A laptop webcam in a dim
  room is a harder problem, and this evaluation does not measure it.
- Static letters only. Fingerspelling is not the same thing as signing ASL, which is a
  language with its own grammar, and a letter classifier is not a translator.
- **MediaPipe labels almost every hand in this dataset the same way** — 950 of 956 in a
  sample come back as one handedness. The feature transform mirrors left onto right, and
  `ml/tests/test_features.py` proves that mapping is exact, so the other hand is handled by
  construction. But it is handled by a proof about the transform, not by evidence from data.
- **Nobody has measured this on a real webcam.** Attempts to simulate one by compositing
  crops into a full frame measured the compositing rather than the camera; see
  `docs/framing-experiment.md`. What that work did establish is that a hand smaller than
  about a third of the frame is where detection starts to fall away, which is why the page
  says so.
